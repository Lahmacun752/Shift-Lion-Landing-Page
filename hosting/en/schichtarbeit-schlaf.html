Why this matters

Changing shifts affect sleep, energy, appointments and time with other people. A clear plan makes the next step easier to see.

Practical tips

Darken the room

Blackout curtains, an eye mask and fewer notifications can improve daytime sleep.

Keep repeatable routines

Similar steps before sleep help your body recognize that the workday is over.

Manage light

Seek bright light when you need to wake up and reduce it before your planned sleep.

Time caffeine

Use caffeine as a tool early in the shift, not as a replacement for recovery.

Plan sleep with your roster

Seeing upcoming shifts early makes naps, appointments and recovery easier to coordinate.

Frequently asked questions

Do these tips work for rotating schedules?

Yes. Adapt them to your shift sequence, personal health and recovery needs.

Can planning reduce shift-work stress?

A clear overview cannot remove every burden, but it can reduce uncertainty and last-minute conflicts.
